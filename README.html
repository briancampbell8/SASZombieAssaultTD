Closing Notes

The modern rebuild of SAS: Zombie Assault TD is more than a technical project—it is a preservation effort, a restoration of a classic game that would otherwise be lost to time. Flash’s end-of-life marked the disappearance of thousands of games, many of which remain inaccessible today. This project stands as a counterexample: a demonstration that classic games can be preserved, rebuilt, and modernized without sacrificing their identity.

Every system in the engine—from timing and rendering to UI and gameplay—has been designed with clarity, determinism, and longevity in mind. The goal is not only to recreate the original experience, but to ensure that it remains playable for decades, independent of any single framework, platform, or technology.

The Preservation Mission

Classic games are cultural artifacts. They represent the creativity, experimentation, and technological limitations of their time. Preserving them is not just about nostalgia—it is about maintaining access to the history of interactive media. The rebuild of SAS: Zombie Assault TD is part of a broader mission to ensure that important works from the Flash era are not lost.

This project demonstrates that preservation does not require emulation or fragile compatibility layers. With careful engineering, classic games can be rebuilt in a way that is faithful, maintainable, and future-proof.

Future Direction

While the primary goal of the rebuild is preservation, the engine’s modular design opens the door to future enhancements and expansions. These possibilities include:

  • New Maps — additional levels built using the existing data-driven map format
  • New Towers and Enemies — easily added through metadata and asset updates
  • Improved Visuals — optional enhancements that do not alter gameplay balance
  • Modding Tools — empowering players to create and share custom content
  • Platform Ports — bringing the game to new devices through the engine’s framework independence

None of these enhancements are required for the preservation mission, but the engine is designed to support them without compromising the authenticity of the original experience.

A Game Worth Preserving

SAS: Zombie Assault TD remains a standout example of Flash-era tower defense design—simple, strategic, and endlessly replayable. This rebuild ensures that the game can continue to be enjoyed by players old and new, without the fragility and limitations of its original platform.

The work done here is not just a technical achievement—it is a commitment to preserving a piece of gaming history.
